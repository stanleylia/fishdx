## Clinical presentation

Epizootic Ulcerative Syndrome (EUS), also known as red-spot disease or mycotic granulomatosis, is caused by the oomycete *Aphanomyces invadans*. Affected freshwater and estuarine fish develop large dermal ulcers with haemorrhagic red lesions and deep necrotic margins extending into underlying musculature. Early lesions appear as small red spots on the flank or caudal peduncle; advanced cases exhibit extensive ulceration, granulomatous invasion, and frequent secondary bacterial infection. Outbreaks are triggered by low water temperature (18–22 °C) combined with low salinity and acidic pH following heavy rain.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with EUS shows large red sores or open wounds on its body, often on the side or near the tail. The wounds look raw, deep, and may have a reddish or bloody appearance. The skin around the sore looks damaged and may be missing scales.
