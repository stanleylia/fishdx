## Clinical presentation

Aeromoniasis, typically presenting as Motile Aeromonas Septicaemia (MAS), is caused by motile *Aeromonas* species — principally *A. hydrophila*, *A. veronii*, and *A. sobria*. Clinical signs are dominated by haemorrhagic septicaemia: petechial and ecchymotic skin haemorrhages, exophthalmia ("pop-eye"), abdominal distension due to ascites, focal skin ulcers, tail-fin erosion, and reddening at the base of the fins. It is frequently an opportunistic infection that follows handling stress, poor water quality, or concurrent parasitism. Mortality in acute outbreaks can reach 80 % within several days.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with Aeromoniasis often looks bloated or swollen in the belly. The eyes may bulge out (pop-eye look). Small skin lesions, red marks at the base of the fins, and a reddish tint to the scales are common. The fish appears tired and swims slowly.
