## Clinical presentation

Bacterial Gill Disease (BGD) is associated primarily with *Flavobacterium branchiophilum* and, in overlapping presentations, *Flavobacterium columnare* (columnaris disease). Affected fish exhibit respiratory distress, surface swimming, flared opercula, and loss of appetite. Gross gill pathology includes pale or mottled gills with excessive mucus; histopathology shows lamellar hyperplasia, fusion, and clubbing with filamentous bacterial colonies at the surface. Yellow-grey mucoid plaques on skin or gills (saddleback lesions) are pathognomonic for columnaris. Predisposing factors include high stocking density, low dissolved oxygen, and elevated organic load.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with bacterial gill disease has gills that look pale, mottled, or damaged instead of bright red. The fish gasps at the water surface, breathes quickly, and the gill covers may flare open. Yellowish or grey patches may appear on the body or gills.
