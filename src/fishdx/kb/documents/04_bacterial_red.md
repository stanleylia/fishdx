## Clinical presentation

Bacterial Red Disease in South Asian freshwater aquaculture is a broad clinical category covering presentations commonly attributable to *Yersinia ruckeri* (enteric redmouth disease, ERM) and to *Vibrio* spp. in brackish settings. Cardinal signs are generalised reddening of the skin, haemorrhages at the base of the fins, reddened mouth and opercular margins, and occasionally reddened vent. Internally: haemorrhagic enteritis, splenomegaly, and pericardial haemorrhage. Mortality is variable; early antibiotic intervention is typically curative. Vaccines exist for *Y. ruckeri* in salmonid aquaculture.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with bacterial red disease shows widespread reddening on its body, mouth, or gills, as if the fish has bled into its skin. Red marks or red patches at the base of the fins are common. The mouth area may look red and swollen.
