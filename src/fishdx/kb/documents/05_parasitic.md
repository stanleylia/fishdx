## Clinical presentation

Common parasitic infections in South Asian freshwater aquaculture span protozoans and metazoans. *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* (Ich, white-spot disease) produces discrete pinhead-sized white cysts on skin and fins with intense flashing and rubbing behaviour. *Trichodina* spp. cause grey slime, frayed fins, and surface flashing under poor water quality. Monogeneans such as *Dactylogyrus* attach to gills causing irritation, excess mucus, and gill haemorrhage. Macroscopic crustacean parasites (*Argulus*, *Lernaea*) are visible as attached fish lice or anchor worms with surrounding inflammation. Clinical triage relies on wet-mount microscopy of skin / gill scrapes.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with parasites often shows tiny white spots on its skin and fins, or visible small organisms attached to the body. The fish may rub against objects, swim erratically (flashing), and lose scales. Small worm-like or insect-like attachments on the body are typical.
