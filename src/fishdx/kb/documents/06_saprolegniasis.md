## Clinical presentation

Saprolegniasis (often called cotton-wool disease) is caused by oomycetes of genus *Saprolegnia*, most notably *S. parasitica* and *S. diclina*. The pathognomonic lesion is a cotton-wool-like white, grey, or brown mycelial tuft on skin, fins, gills, eyes, or fish eggs. Infection typically colonises existing injuries or stress-damaged tissue; temperature drops, handling abrasions, and egg-stage exposure are principal triggers. Lesions are frequently annular or patchy; secondary bacterial invasion is common. Malachite green treatment is classical but increasingly restricted; formalin, salt, or hydrogen peroxide are current alternatives.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with Saprolegniasis has visible white or grey fluffy patches on its skin, fins, or eyes — they look like cotton wool or fuzz growing on the fish. The patches typically grow on existing wounds or damaged skin and may spread quickly.
