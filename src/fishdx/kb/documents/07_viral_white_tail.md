## Clinical presentation

Viral White Tail Disease is a clinical umbrella for viral infections that present with progressive whitening and translucency of the caudal musculature. In freshwater cyprinid and catfish aquaculture, associations include rhabdoviruses and nodaviruses; in prawn farming "white tail disease" is classically associated with Macrobrachium rosenbergii nodavirus (MrNV), though that context is distinct from finfish. Clinically, fish show loss of pigment on the tail and caudal peduncle, progressive white or pale-translucent musculature, loss of swimming coordination, spiral swimming or vertical flotation, and behavioural lethargy. Mortality is typically high in fry and fingerling stages; PCR confirmation is required for etiological certainty.

## Lay-visible appearance

A fish with viral white tail disease shows a whitened or pale tail and tail-fin region, while the rest of the body keeps normal colour. The whitening may extend up the body. The fish swims oddly — sideways, in spirals, or floats vertically — and may stop responding to movement.
